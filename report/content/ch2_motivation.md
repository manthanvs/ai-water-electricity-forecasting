# Motivation and Need of the Study

## Operational Need

When supply is planned on averages, every departure from the average has a cost. A water utility that cannot anticipate tomorrow's demand keeps its reservoirs high and pumps whenever the level falls, regardless of the price of electricity at that hour. An electricity distribution company that underestimates load must buy power at short notice, usually at a higher price. Leaks are often found only after large volumes have been lost [@kingdom2006nrw]. Accurate next-day and next-week forecasts reduce each of these costs.

## The Water-Energy Link

Water supply is itself an electrical load. Reservoirs are commonly filled by pumps that switch on when the level reaches a minimum and off at a maximum; if the demand of the coming hours is known, the same pumps can run when energy is cheaper, and the utility saves money over time [@antunes2018]. A better water forecast is therefore also an electricity saving, and studying both resources together shows how forecasting connects them.

## Unused Smart Meter Data

Smart meters produce fine-grained data whose analytical value for load analysis, forecasting and demand management is still being developed [@wang2019smartmeter]. In India the roll-out is recent, and the benefits reported so far concern real-time readings and billing efficiency [@pib2025smartmeters]. The methods studied here show what such data can offer once they are also used for prediction.

## Expected Benefits

- Reliable next-day hourly and next-week daily forecasts of water demand and electricity consumption.
- Early detection of abnormal spikes, leakage and wastage from forecast residuals.
- Better allocation of pumping, storage and purchased power.
- Lower operating cost through tariff-aware scheduling.
- Measurable progress towards the sustainable use of both resources.
