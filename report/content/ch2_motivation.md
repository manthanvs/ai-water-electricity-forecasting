# Motivation and Need of the Study

## Operational Need

When supply is planned on averages, every departure from the average has a cost. A water utility that cannot anticipate tomorrow's demand pumps whenever the reservoir level falls, whatever the price of electricity at that hour. An electricity distribution company that underestimates load must buy power at short notice. Leaks are often found only after large volumes have been lost [@kingdom2006nrw]. Accurate next-day and next-week forecasts reduce each of these costs.

## The Water-Energy Link

Water supply is itself an electrical load. Reservoirs are commonly filled by pumps that switch on at a minimum level and off at a maximum; if the coming demand is known, the pumps can run when energy is cheaper [@antunes2018]. A better water forecast is therefore also an electricity saving.

## Unused Smart Meter Data

The value of smart-meter data for load analysis, forecasting and demand management is still being developed [@wang2019smartmeter], and the benefits reported for India so far concern real-time readings and billing [@pib2025smartmeters]. This study shows what such data can offer for prediction.

## Expected Benefits

- Reliable next-day hourly and next-week daily forecasts of water demand and electricity consumption.
- Early detection of abnormal spikes, leakage and wastage from forecast residuals.
- Better allocation of pumping, storage and purchased power, and lower operating cost.
- Measurable progress towards the sustainable use of both resources.
